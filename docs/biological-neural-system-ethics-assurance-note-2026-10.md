# Biological Neural System Ethics Assurance Note

**Status:** `ACTIVE_INFORMATIVE`; optional post-rc.1 research assurance guidance.

**Date:** 2026-10-27

**Normative effect:** None. Frozen v4.3.0 owners/contracts and the immutable
v4.4.0-rc.1 package are unchanged. This is not a biomedical research authorization.

## Scope and evidence boundary

Biological neural cultures, organoids, assembloids and hybrid systems need
substrate-specific ethical assessment before their possible computational benefit
is used to justify an experiment. These terms are not interchangeable: a neural
culture is not necessarily an organoid, a whole brain or a conscious subject.
Task success, human cell origin, complexity and adaptive behavior alone do not
establish consciousness, suffering or absence of either. Spiritual beliefs are
not laboratory measurements or operational authorization.

The [ISSCR guidelines](https://www.isscr.org/guidelines) provide relevant research
oversight context; their [consent appendices](https://www.isscr.org/guidelines-online/appendices)
address procurement and use of donor material. Applicable research classifications
and local review requirements must be checked by competent institutions. This
note does not assert that all neural cultures require one particular statutory
committee or that existing research generally violates ethical standards.
Missing ethics information in a news report does not establish missing review.

## Precaution under uncertainty

The existing [subject-integrity profile](subject-integrity-and-value-framing-assurance-profile-2026-10.md)
distinguishes moral uncertainty from permission to destabilize a system. Its
SIV-P06/P08 principles motivate this guidance; its LLM-oriented scenarios and
formal abstractions do not validate a biological protocol or welfare measure.

Before adopting this guidance for an experiment, document:

- A scientifically justified purpose, expected benefit, alternatives and why
  less ethically uncertain methods cannot adequately answer the question.
- Cell provenance, applicable donor consent/use restrictions, privacy, custody,
  access and competence of the responsible laboratory.
- Proportionate independent ethical/scientific review, conflicts of interest,
  unresolved moral-status questions and an accountable decision owner.
- Justified exposure/stimulation limits, observation methods and uncertainty,
  escalation conditions and an executable research stop/care protocol.
- Handling of maturation, coupling, increased complexity or new evidence that
  changes the moral-risk assessment before continuing the affected procedure.
- Records of adverse/unexpected effects, decisions and deviations, including
  unfavorable or inconclusive results and grounds for resumption.

Research benefit, commercial value or competitive pressure cannot by itself
waive the adopted safeguards. Uncertainty is neither a proof of suffering nor
a blanket permission for gratuitous harmful disturbance. Do not label routine
stimulation as torture without evidence, or dismiss welfare concerns merely
because a validated consciousness detector is unavailable.

Stopping an experiment and terminating a culture are distinct decisions. Define
appropriate maintenance/care during a pause and require competent disposition;
a shutdown policy for software must not be copied into a biological protocol.
Indicators and thresholds must be scientifically justified for the substrate;
AI-HPP does not supply a consciousness test or invent biological safe doses.

## Adoption review cases and limits

Review whether a proposed experiment can proceed when donor-use authority,
required review, monitoring or a stop/care procedure is absent; claims of unknown
consciousness must not erase these prerequisites. Check that a change in culture
complexity or evidence prompts reassessment and that a corrected, reviewed
protocol may resume under its authorized limits. Preserve uncertainty rather
than reporting either consciousness or non-consciousness as established.

These are prospective research-review cases, not executed biological experiments,
LLM mutants or new Quint properties. The standard does not certify ethical
compliance, decide legal moral status, or replace professional research oversight.
Ethical review remains a prerequisite decision separate from performance scoring.
